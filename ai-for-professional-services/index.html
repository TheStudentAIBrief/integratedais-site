Professional services

AI for professional services

Law firms, accountancy practices and consultancies hold exactly the data that makes AI valuable — client matters, advice, working papers — and exactly the confidentiality obligations that make a public AI service impossible. Integrated AIS helps them adopt AI inside their own confidentiality boundary, so the capability arrives without client matter data ever leaving their control.

Discuss a confidential deployment

The professional services constraint

A professional firm's entire relationship with its clients rests on confidentiality, and in legal work on legal privilege — protections that a hosted AI API cannot satisfy on its own. Client files, advice and working papers cannot be sent to a third-party model provider, and a contractual assurance rarely satisfies a risk function that has to evidence control rather than be promised it. Professional obligations set by your regulator, and the duty to manage conflicts between clients, apply to an AI system just as they do to any other 《CONFIRM: specific SRA / ICAEW rules relevant to the firm and matter》.

There is also the question of where client matter data resides: for many clients, data residency is a condition of the engagement, not a preference. The result is that many firms have stalled on AI — not for lack of use cases, but because every credible one ran into a confidentiality wall. The way through is not to weaken the duty; it is to bring the model inside the boundary that already protects the client.

Where AI earns its place

The most durable value in a professional firm is in the knowledge- and document-heavy work that fills the working day: retrieving and summarising internal knowledge and precedent, drafting and reviewing documentation, accelerating research and analysis over material that cannot leave the firm, and cutting manual effort out of controlled workflows. These reduce load on fee earners and support teams and carry manageable risk.

We are deliberately cautious about anything that is presented as advice or a professional judgement, where the right design keeps a qualified professional accountable and uses AI to assist rather than to decide — and about conflicts, where matter-level separation may be required. Knowing which side of that line a use case sits on is part of the work, not an afterthought.

How we deliver it

We assess where AI genuinely helps against your practice and risk appetite; deploy it securely inside your infrastructure — on-premises or in a private, in-region environment that satisfies client data-residency requirements — with the data boundaries, access controls and logging your risk and compliance function can evidence; and design in the matter-level separation and human oversight that confidentiality, privilege and conflicts require. Because we are vendor-neutral, the architecture is shaped by your obligations rather than by a product we need you to license.

This draws on ourstrategy, integration and secure deployment capabilities, and on the sameon-premises anddata-sovereign deployment patterns we build for other confidentiality-critical sectors.

Common questions

Can a professional services firm use AI without sending client matter data to the cloud?

Yes. Open-weight models can be deployed on-premises or in a private, in-region environment so that client files, matter data and privileged material never leave the boundary your confidentiality obligations already cover. The model runs inside your own controls, which means adopting AI does not require sending confidential client information to a third-party provider — the difference between an AI programme your risk and compliance function will approve and one it will refuse.

How does AI fit with confidentiality, privilege and professional obligations?

It has to fit the duties you already owe. Client confidentiality and, in legal work, legal privilege are non-negotiable, and professional obligations set by your regulator apply to an AI system exactly as they apply to any other 《CONFIRM: specific SRA / ICAEW rules relevant to the firm and matter》. We design deployments with those obligations in mind from the start — documented data boundaries, access controls and logging your risk function can evidence, matter-level separation where conflicts require it, and human oversight wherever an output informs advice.
